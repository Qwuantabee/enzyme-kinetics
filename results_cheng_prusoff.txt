------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(b) what Cheng-Prusoff implies, and what assumption reconciles it
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a competitive inhibitor, IC50 = Ki (1 + [S]/Km). Inverting the
observed ratio gives the assay's substrate factor directly:

  observed ratio    implied [S]/Km
            1.00              0.00
            2.00              1.00
            9.38              8.38
            3.04              2.04
           20.95             19.95

Per-group median ratios run from 3.0 to 20.9, i.e. implied [S]/Km from
2.0 to 19.9. The pooled median (9.4) is an average over those and
is not a conversion factor for any one assay.

The assumptions behind reading a ratio as an assay factor: every pair
is a competitive inhibitor; each group ran a single [S]; and each IC50
is a true 50% inhibition point rather than a fitted midpoint off a
partial curve. Relax any of them and the ratio stops being an assay
constant. Nothing in these files constrains [S] for most assays, so for
those no pair can confirm or refute the correction it implies.

Direct (1 + [S]/Km) check: compounds measured at two stated [S]
  11 compounds in CHEMBL1136127 have IC50 at 30 and 100 uM
  substrate (assay descriptions); their Ki assay description(s): ['Inhibition constant against Dihydrofolate reductase', 'Inhibition constant of compound against dihydrofolate reductase']
  (no [S] stated for the Ki).
  Cheng-Prusoff bounds IC50(100)/IC50(30) between 1 (Km >> S) and 3.33 (Km << S).
  Measured ratio: median 2.18, range 1.45 to 2.77; 11 of 11 inside the bounds.
  Km from the IC50 pair alone (Ki-free), median: 29.2 uM.
  Km from each single condition's IC50/Ki ratio, median: 8.1 uM at 30 uM, 11.4 uM at 100 uM.
  If Km were the single-condition value, the predicted IC50 ratio would be 2.84 (30-based Km) or 2.69 (100-based Km),
  against the measured median 2.18. Per-compound values are in results/cheng_prusoff_two_substrate.csv.
  The pair-derived Km is noisy (it divides by rho - 1 and rho is near 1 for some compounds),
  so its median is a coarse number; both Km estimates are reported as measured, with no interval.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(c) assay conditions vs genuinely different compounds
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same-publication pairs are the tighter comparison: one lab, one
protocol family, one enzyme preparation. Same-molecule pairs that span
publications mix all of those, so their scatter has more in it than
assay variation alone.

log10(ratio) variance decomposition, strict pairs (>=3 pairs/group):
  total   0.1707
  between 0.0876  (51.3% of total)
  within  0.0831  (48.7%)
  5 groups, 46 pairs
  one-way ANOVA: F(4,41) = 12.86, p = 7.2e-07
  same test, excl. antibacterial-described Ki: F(3,35) = 15.58, p = 1.3e-06, 53.5% explained
  same test, IC50 pooled over [S]: F(3,31) = 13.69, p = 7.4e-06, 52.8% explained

Between-group differences are a 2.0-fold effect on the ratio (SD-scale);
within-group scatter is 1.9-fold. The ANOVA finds the group means different (p = 7.2e-07), and 51% of the variance is attributable
to which paper/condition a pair came from. That is the pattern a
per-assay [S]/Km would produce; any per-assay systematic difference
would produce it too, so it does not single out Cheng-Prusoff from a
rival explanation.

Does the ratio track potency within a group? Slope of log10(ratio) on
log10(IC50), with the 95% t-interval; and on log10(Ki). The response
shares a variable with both regressors, so independent noise biases
the IC50 slope upward and the Ki slope downward: read the interval and
the pair of signs, not the point estimate alone.

  CHEMBL3632554          n=14  slope(IC50)=-0.025 [-0.070,+0.020] p=0.26  slope(Ki)=-0.029 p=0.17  IC50 span 3.1 decades  [enzyme Ki]
  CHEMBL1136127@30uM     n=11  slope(IC50)=-0.109 [-0.319,+0.101] p=0.27  slope(Ki)=-0.152 p=0.06  IC50 span 3.4 decades  [enzyme Ki]

  CHEMBL1124568          n= 7  slope(IC50)=+0.602 [-0.094,+1.298] p=0.08  slope(Ki)=-0.242 p=0.66  IC50 span 1.1 decades  [antibacterial-described Ki]

4 of 4 groups with >=5 pairs have a slope interval that includes 0; none excludes 0.
An interval that includes 0 is the absence of a detected trend, not
evidence of none: groups of 7-14 points have wide intervals, and
the shortest IC50 span here is 1.1 decades.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(d) verdict
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT (strict join): partially supported
  - within-paper log10 SD 0.29 (1.9-fold, limit 0.30): ratios are reproducible inside a publication
  - between-paper difference significant at alpha 0.05: ANOVA F(4,41) = 12.86, p = 7.2e-07; papers account for 51% of the log-ratio variance
  - no assay [S] or [E] is recorded for most assays, so the absolute conversion is untested compound by compound: consistency is necessary, not sufficient
verdict (loose join): not testable
  - no paper contributes enough pairs to measure a within-assay spread, so the ratios cannot be shown to be an assay constant or to differ between assays
verdict (excl. antibacterial-desc.): partially supported
  - within-paper log10 SD 0.29 (1.9-fold, limit 0.30): ratios are reproducible inside a publication
verdict (IC50 pooled over [S]): partially supported
  - within-paper log10 SD 0.28 (1.9-fold, limit 0.30): ratios are reproducible inside a publication

Threshold sensitivity: the pooled within-group log10 SD is 0.288 against the fixed
limit 0.30; with a limit of 0.25 the strict-join label would be 'unsupported'.

What the label means: the ratio is reproducible inside a publication to
within the stated limit and the groups differ (F-test above); that is the
structure Cheng-Prusoff requires, and support for the relation's *form*.
It is not validation of its *values*: for most assays the file that would
settle those -- one carrying [S] alongside IC50 and Ki -- is not here.

Qualifications from the numbers: 2 pairs fall below the model's floor of ratio 1;
the pooled within-group SD is 1.9-fold, and per-group max/min ratio spreads run
from 2.0x to 26.9x (table above), so the ratio is a per-group constant
with real noise on it rather than a tight one.

What is missing, stated exactly:
  - assay substrate concentration [S] (stated for only some IC50 assays, in free text)
  - assay enzyme concentration [E]
  - the Km of the assay's substrate under the assay's conditions

With [S] and the assay Km, pairing.assay_residuals computes the
predicted ratio and the per-compound log residual directly; that is
the check this data cannot run for most assays and a suitable paper
could.
