------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(b) what Cheng-Prusoff implies, and what assumption reconciles it
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a competitive inhibitor, IC50 = Ki (1 + [S]/Km). Inverting the
observed ratio gives the assay's substrate factor directly:

  observed ratio    implied [S]/Km
            1.00              0.00
            2.00              1.00
            7.47              6.47
           21.00             20.00
           30.00             29.00

Quoting one implied [S]/Km for the whole set would be misleading, and
section (a) is the reason. The pooled median of 7.5 is an average
over papers whose own median ratios run from 3.0 to 20.9,
i.e. implied [S]/Km from about 2.0 to 20.
Quoted per paper the factor is a concrete number of the size
inhibition assays of this enzyme plausibly use; quoted across the
whole set it is an average of incompatible things and means little.

A single global [S]/Km CANNOT reconcile this data, and that is worth
stating plainly: with one factor, every paper's median ratio would
have to be the same number. They are not. What reconciles the data is
a per-assay factor -- which is exactly what Cheng-Prusoff says the
ratio is. So the median is reported because it summarises the set, not
because it is the conversion factor.

The assumptions that make that per-assay reading consistent: every
pair is a competitive inhibitor; each paper ran a single [S]; and each
IC50 is a true 50% inhibition point rather than a fitted midpoint off a
partial curve. Relax any of them and the ratio stops being an assay
constant.

The assumption is NOT independently checkable here. Nothing in these
files constrains [S], so no pair can confirm or refute the correction it
implies. That is the ceiling of this test.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(c) assay conditions vs genuinely different compounds
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same-publication pairs are the tighter comparison: one lab, one
protocol family, one enzyme preparation, and typically one substrate
concentration. Same-molecule pairs that span publications mix all of
those, so their scatter has more in it than assay variation alone.

log10(ratio) variance decomposition, strict pairs (>=3 pairs/paper):
  total   0.1701
  between 0.0898  (52.8% of total)
  within  0.0802  (47.2%)
  4 papers, 35 pairs

Between-paper differences are a 2.0-fold effect on the ratio;
within-paper scatter is 1.9-fold. So roughly half the total
variance in the ratio is 'which paper this came from', i.e. assay
conditions, not 'which compound this is'. That is the pattern
Cheng-Prusoff predicts. It is also what any per-assay systematic
difference would produce, so it discriminates the conversion from
noise, not from a rival theory.

Rejecting the alternative: if the ratio were really a per-compound
correction rather than a per-assay constant, it would track potency
within a paper. Fitting log10(ratio) on log10(IC50) per document:

Per paper (>=5 pairs), with the IC50 range each slope was fitted over:
  CHEMBL3632554    n=14  slope=-0.025  r=-0.33  1.15e+03-1.55e+06  [enzyme]
  CHEMBL1136127    n=11  slope=-0.117  r=-0.39      25.8-7.59e+04  [enzyme]
  CHEMBL1124568    n= 7  slope=+0.602  r=+0.71       1.2-15        [whole-cell]

2 of 3 papers with >=5 pairs have |slope| < 0.2 while
spanning 2-3 orders of magnitude of potency: the ratio does not track
potency, so it is not a per-compound correction. The exception is the
whole-cell paper (CHEMBL1124568), whose 'Ki' is antibacterial potency
rather than a binding constant -- pairing it with a purified-enzyme
IC50 compares two different quantities, which is why the enzyme-only
column exists.

Restricting to enzyme-assay Ki records (29 pairs) moves the
median to 14.74 and the log10 SD to 0.410. Both are reported;
the cell-based rows are not deleted, because dropping data to improve
a statistic is how a validation becomes a demonstration.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(d) verdict
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT: partially supported
  - within-paper log10 SD 0.28 (1.9-fold): ratios are reproducible inside a publication
  - 53% of the log-ratio variance is between papers (2.0-fold from assay conditions), as a per-assay [S]/Km requires
  - but no assay [S] or [E] is recorded, so the absolute conversion is untested compound by compound: consistency is necessary, not sufficient

In plain terms: the ratio is reproducible within a paper and differs
between papers, which is the structure Cheng-Prusoff requires. That is
real support for the conversion's *form*. It is not validation of the
conversion's *values*: the file that would settle those -- one carrying
[S] alongside IC50 and Ki -- is not in this repository.

Nor is the support unqualified. 2 pairs fall below the
model's floor of ratio 1, and the pooled within-paper spread is
1.9-fold even in the best paper, so the ratio
is a per-assay constant with real noise on it rather than a tight one.
Both facts are in the tables above instead of being smoothed away: a
validation that hides its own residuals is a demonstration.

What is missing, stated exactly:
  - assay substrate concentration [S]
  - assay enzyme concentration [E]
  - the Km of the assay's substrate under the assay's conditions

With [S] and the assay Km, `pairing.assay_residuals` computes the
predicted ratio and the per-compound log residual directly; that is
the check this data cannot run and a suitable paper could.
