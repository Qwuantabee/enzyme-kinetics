--- what this establishes ---
Each row above is a real measured affinity next to real coordinates:
the number of residues lining the pocket, which residues they are,
how enclosed the ligand is, and how many contacts of each type.
Those are computed from the deposited coordinates and can be checked
against the structure by hand.

--- what it does not establish ---
Not a structure-affinity relationship. With one protein and at most a
few ligands that have both a measured Ki and a crystal structure,
any correlation between burial and potency would be anecdotal, and a
p-value on n=3 would be a decoration. Establishing that a contact
contributes to binding needs mutagenesis or a congeneric series, not
a comparison of two ligands.

The geometric descriptors are also proxies, not physics:
  - burial is a contact-count fraction, not a Shrake-Rupley SASA
  - 'hydrophobic contacts' count carbon-carbon distances, which
    correlates with burial but is not an energy
  - the Ki and the structure may come from different conditions
    (pH, temperature, buffer, construct, and sometimes species)

The last is the one that bites in practice. 4DFR contains both an
E. coli and a Lactobacillus casei copy; analysing the wrong chain
would compare an E. coli Ki to a L. casei pocket and look fine.

6 Ki/structure pairs joined: 1 compound x 2 distinct Ki records x 3 structures.
They are NOT independent observations: the same compound and the same
two Ki values are laid against three coordinate sets of one protein.

Counting rule for 'contacts' above: each count is a number of
(protein atom, ligand atom) PAIRS under a distance cutoff (hbond: N/O
<= 3.5 A; salt_bridge: protein N/O of ARG/LYS/HIS to ligand O, or
ASP/GLU N/O to ligand N, <= 4.0 A, backbone atoms included; no angle or
charge check). They are not counts of distinct interactions.
